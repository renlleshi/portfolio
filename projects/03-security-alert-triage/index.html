Alert fatigue, minus the fatigue

A workflow that takes a raw security alert from any source, explains it in plain language, and only wakes someone up when it's actually serious.

The problem

Small security and IT teams get flooded with alerts from different tools, each in its own format. Reading every one manually is slow, and the alerts that actually matter get buried under low-value noise.

How it works

  • Accept alerts from anywhere

    A webhook receives alerts from a SIEM tool, a script, or a manual report form — whatever the source.

  • Normalize the shape

    Different tools send different fields; this step maps everything to one consistent structure.

  • Classify with an LLM — carefully

    The model assigns a severity, writes a plain-language summary, and suggests a next action, instructed not to invent facts beyond what the alert actually contains.

  • Keep a full audit trail

    Every alert, regardless of severity, is logged to a sheet — nothing disappears silently.

  • Escalate only what's serious

    High and critical alerts are pushed straight to a Slack security channel; everything else waits in the log.

Why it matters

This is the project closest to my background: it's not just wiring tool A to tool B, it's reducing alert fatigue while being honest about what the AI does and doesn't know about an incident.
